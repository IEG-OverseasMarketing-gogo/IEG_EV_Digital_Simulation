Instructor Guide — Warehouse Automation (AMR/WMS) Module

Companion to: IEG 6D Digital Twin — AMR/WMS Prototype · IEG LMS Instructor Dashboard Prerequisite: Forklift certification (BluWorkz REAL-Forklift, MSSC CLA-aligned) Total course time: 4 sessions × 45–50 min (can compress to a single 3-hour block for short-format cohorts) Class size supported: Up to 20 stations, monitored live from one instructor dashboard

Course-level objectives

By the end of this module, a student who has already completed forklift training should be able to:

  1. Explain the difference between AGV and AMR systems and identify the four core zones of an automated warehouse
  2. Dispatch and monitor an AMR through a simulated pick task
  3. Apply correct floor-safety judgment in an automation-specific conflict (aisle congestion)
  4. Diagnose a WMS inventory-mismatch alert using a structured troubleshooting sequence

Each objective maps to a named MSSC CLT work activity — see mssc-competency-mapping.md for the full table.

Session 1 — Principle (45 min)

Time Activity
0–5 min Warm-up: ask students to name every step a forklift operator takes from receiving to shipping. Write it on the board.
5–15 min Instructor-led walkthrough of the four warehouse zones using the Principle tab. Click each zone card live; have students predict the content before you reveal it.
15–25 min AGV vs. AMR comparison — use the two boxes in the demo as a starting point, then ask: "Which would you choose for a warehouse that rearranges its layout every season, and why?"
25–35 min Introduce the i-MOBA lineup (standard / manipulator / conveyor). Ask students to guess which type pairs best with forklift skills before revealing the ★ recommended pairing — this primes the "smallest skill jump" framing for Session 2.
35–45 min Individual station time: students open the Principle tab themselves and click through all four zone cards. Instructor monitors via the LMS dashboard.

Common error to watch for: Students often confuse AS/RS (a storage system) with AMR (a mobile robot) — they're not the same category. Address this explicitly; it resurfaces in Session 4's WMS scenario.

Session 2 — Equipment Operation (45 min)

Time Activity
0–5 min Recap: which i-MOBA type did we recommend pairing with forklift skills, and why?
5–15 min Instructor demo of the dispatch exercise on the shared screen — narrate what the WMS sync log is doing under the hood.
15–35 min Individual station time: each student dispatches to all three racks (A/B/C) at least once.
35–45 min Debrief: what does "WMS synced" actually confirm? (Physical location and system record now agree — this is the concept Session 4 tests when that agreement breaks.)

Common error to watch for: Students treat the dispatch task as a game to finish quickly rather than reading the log output. Cold-call 2–3 students to read their log back to the class before moving on.

Session 3 — Hands-On Scenario (45 min)

Time Activity
0–10 min Discuss: "What would you do if two forklifts approached the same aisle at once?" Bridge to the AMR-equivalent conflict.
10–20 min Individual station time: students work through the aisle-conflict scenario independently first, before seeing the group answer.
20–35 min Group discussion of the three choices. Ask students to defend the wrong answers out loud — this surfaces the actual reasoning error (usually: treating an automated system like a manual one).
35–45 min Instructor-led: connect back to CLA safety principles (already certified) and show how they extend, not disappear, once equipment is automated.

Live dashboard note: This is the session where "idle 6+ minutes" flags are most common — students freeze when the obvious manual-intervention answer is wrong. Have a check-in script ready (see below).

Check-in script for a stalled station: "Walk me through why you'd want to physically move the robots yourself. What's the WMS already doing that a person can't see?" — don't give the answer, redirect to the sensor/priority-conflict framing.

Session 4 — WMS Troubleshooting (50 min)

Time Activity
0–5 min Show the ALERT #4471 banner on the shared screen. Ask: "What's your instinct — check something, or restart something?"
5–10 min Do not let students start yet. This is the highest-failure step (62% of a typical class picks "restart the WMS server" first — see LMS dashboard sticking-point data). Run a short group walkthrough of why root-cause checking comes before any reset action.
10–35 min Individual station time: full two-step diagnostic sequence.
35–45 min Group review: what did the pending-transfer discrepancy represent in real terms? Tie back to CLT work activity #5 (Inventory control) and #9 (Measurements and metric conversions).
45–50 min Certification quiz (see assessment-rubric.md) and tier badge issuance.

Common error to watch for: Students who get Step 1 wrong often repeat the same instinct at Step 2 (editing the count manually instead of confirming the transfer) — flag these students individually rather than assuming the group debrief fixed it for everyone.

Materials checklist

  • [ ] One browser-capable station per student (up to 20), instructor device with LMS dashboard open
  • [ ] Projector/shared screen for instructor-led segments
  • [ ] Printed or digital Reference Guide (see reference-guide.md) available during Sessions 3–4
  • [ ] Assessment rubric and answer key (assessment-rubric.md) — instructor-only, not distributed to students
